# CareTogether_demo
CareTogether: Bridging the Language Gap in Elder Care

## Project Background
This project began at an Entrepreneurship Program we joined in Singapore. Our team was assigned the theme “Caring for Aging Parents” — a problem that sounds familiar at first glance, but is surprisingly hard to define in just a few words. Instead of jumping straight to a solution, we decided to spend time breaking the problem down: Where exactly does elder care fall short? Who are the people on the front lines of caregiving? And what do they actually struggle with every day?

## The Pain Point
According to the data we researched, Taiwan has 873,000 migrant workers, nearly 200,000 of whom work as home caregivers, coming from Indonesia, Vietnam, the Philippines, and Thailand. A survey by Taiwan’s Ministry of Labor found that 35% of households reported difficulties getting along with foreign caregivers, with 72.3% of those difficulties stemming from language communication.

This led us to the pain point we chose to focus on: the issue isn’t a lack of caregiving ability — it’s the absence of a clear, real-time bridge of communication between the two sides.

Families hesitate to trust the care of their parents to a caregiver they can’t communicate with, while caregivers often make mistakes or feel distrusted simply because they can’t understand instructions.

So we set out to build a product that creates trust — Care Together.

# Features
The core features are simple and intuitive:

Task Assignment — Families can create daily care tasks in just seconds
Cross-Language Communication — The system automatically translates, so caregivers receive tasks in the language they’re most comfortable with
Automated Care Logs — Every completed task leaves behind a photo and record, so families can check in anytime — and caregivers’ efforts are recognized
Good care isn’t built on surveillance — it’s built on mutual understanding. Care Together removes language as a barrier, giving families peace of mind and giving caregivers the respect they deserve.
